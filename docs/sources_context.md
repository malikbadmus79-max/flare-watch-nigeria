# Context sources

These sources give context only. They are not used in any calculation. They are cited on the dashboard's Methods page, one short line each. All were accessed on 2026-11-20 and recorded without re-checking them online.

| # | Context | Source | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The NOSDRA gas flare tracker has not been updated since May 2025. | SweetCrude Reports, 30 Nov 2025, "Gas flare worsens as NOSDRA discontinues monitoring" | https://sweetcrudereports.com/gas-flare-worsens-as-nosdra-discontinues-monitoring/ |
| 2 | NUPRC has set a target to end routine gas flaring by 2030. | NAN (News Agency of Nigeria), 28 Oct 2026, "Nigeria to end routine gas flaring by 2030 – NUPRC" | https://nannews.ng/nigeria-to-end-routine-gas-flaring-by-2030-nuprc/ |
| 3 | Nigeria is one of nine countries that together accounted for 83% of global flare volumes in 2025. | World Bank, *Global Gas Flaring Tracker*, June 2026 (`data/raw/docs/Global-Gas-FlaringTracker-June-23-2026.pdf`): PDF p.18, "these top nine flaring countries were responsible for 83 percent of global flare volumes". The same point appears in Key Findings, PDF p.11. | (local copy, see `data/interim/phase0_sources.csv`) |

How they are used:
- **Source 2 (NUPRC 2030 target):** this is the background to the dashboard's *illustrative* straight-line path to zero by 2030.
  - The path is not a forecast.
  - The project makes no statement that Nigeria is on or off track.
  - Satellites measure all flaring, not only routine flaring.
- **Source 1 (NOSDRA):** it explains why this project uses the World Bank satellite series rather than a national tracker. The project says nothing about why monitoring stopped.
- **Source 3 (World Bank):** it places Nigeria among the main flaring countries. It is quoted as written.
